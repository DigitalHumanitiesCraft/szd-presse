About this tool

The Aufsatzablage of the Stefan Zweig estate in the Literaturarchiv Salzburg holds Zweig's own collection of press clippings. Its catalogue records where a piece appeared as one line of free text, for example Neue Freie Presse, Wien, 3. 5. 1911, S. 2. As text, the same newspaper can be written in several ways, and two newspapers can share a name.

This tool reads every such line with a deterministic parser into periodical, place, date and page, then joins the lines into periodicals. Case, a leading article and the spelling Litteratur are ignored automatically; a few further joins are editorial decisions, each shown with its reason. Lines the parser cannot read, book references and clippings without a reference stay visible as their own group. Nothing is guessed. Every join is a proposal by rule until a person confirms it; deciding in the tool is the next step of its development. The modelled data can be downloaded under Data.

Data: catalogue of the Aufsatzablage, Stefan Zweig Digital, Literaturarchiv Salzburg (TEI, CC BY-NC 4.0). Facsimiles: Literaturarchiv Salzburg, Stefan Zweig Digital, CC BY 4.0, reduced in size. The tool was built with coding agents following Promptotyping by Digital Humanities Craft.
